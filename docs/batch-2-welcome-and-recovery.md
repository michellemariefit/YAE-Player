### Summary (parent only)

Why your athlete gets hurt, and what this program does about each one:

1. **Weak stabilizer muscles.** Running and practice hammer the big muscles. The small ones around the knee and ankle only get strong if you train them on purpose. The Stability workouts do that.
2. **Weakness in joint positions.** Games put her in bent, twisted, extreme positions her training never visits. Isometric holds and pulses teach her body to be strong there.
3. **Tightness.** More practice, no recovery. Mobility, stretching and rolling, and she learns to do them herself so you don't have to.
4. **Muscle imbalances.** Strong quads, weak hamstrings and glutes. That is where most ACL tears come from. The Foundation workouts load the back of the body on purpose.
5. **Compensation.** When something hurts, she moves differently, other muscles take over, and that is when the real injury happens. Pain that lingers is a stop-and-check, never a play-through.

## Myofascial release (5.5 min)

**Proposed:** about 3 min. The knee explanation is the keeper. The PDF references go, since the recovery page replaces that PDF.

### Summary

Myofascial release is a fancy word for massage. Tight muscles pull on a joint, the joint sits wrong, and running on it like that is how you get hurt. So: do not run through pain. Knee pain usually comes from the inner quad and where the muscles insert at the knee. Roll those first.

Tools, all cheap, all in the sports bag: small foam roller, massage gun, hand roller, tennis or baseball. Pick the one you have.
