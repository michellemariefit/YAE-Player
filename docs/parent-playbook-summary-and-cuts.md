# The Parent Playbook · one-page summary (draft for Michelle)

**Watch time now:** 15.9 min. **Proposed cut:** about 7 min.

## If you only read this

This lesson is for the parent. Your athlete does not need to watch it.

Two jobs for you:

1. **Know what is in the program and why**, so you can explain it when she asks. She can run the workouts herself. Under 10, do it with her for the first few weeks.
2. **Keep her consistent.** Not every kid has the work ethic. That is not the kid's fault and it does not mean she won't make it. Pushing is the parent's job.

## The one line that works

When she does not want to do it, ask one question: **"What's your goal?"**

If the answer is still "play high school" or "play college", then: "Okay. Then this is what it takes. You do it tired. You do it sore."

Say it out loud so she hears it: you are not pushing because you want a star athlete. You are pushing because it is *her* goal. The day she says it is not her goal anymore is the day you stop.

## Make it automatic

- Pick a time and keep it every week. "Whenever there's time" never happens.
- Attach it to something she already does. Practice at 6 on Monday, Wednesday, Friday? Stretching at 5, same days. Home from practice? Mobility before she sits down.
- Use the week planner. Set it up together on Sunday night. If this week's plan didn't work, change it next week. There is no wrong answer, only the one she will actually do.
